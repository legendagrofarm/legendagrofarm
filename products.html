*The CCN-51 Cocoa Planting Guide: High-Density Management* ​The CCN-51 is a high-performance asset. To see a return on your investment within 24 months, you must follow these technical protocols from day one. ​Phase 1: Site Preparation and Shade Management ​Soil Requirements: Cocoa thrives in deep, well-drained soil rich in organic matter. Ensure the site has good water retention but zero waterlogging. ​Temporary Shade: Young cocoa seedlings are sensitive to direct, harsh sunlight. We recommend planting temporary shade crops like plantains or bananas six months before the cocoa. This protects the young CCN-51 and provides a secondary income stream. ​Phase 2: Precise Linear Layout ​The 3m x 3m Grid: Accuracy is vital. Use a lining rope to ensure every tree is exactly 3 meters from its neighbour in a straight line. This creates the "tunnel" effect needed for air circulation and easy harvesting. ​The Hole: Dig holes of 40cm x 40cm x 40cm. ​Soil Priming: Mix the excavated topsoil with 5kg of cured poultry manure and 200g of Phosphate fertiliser. Fill the hole back up slightly so the seedling sits at the correct height. ​Phase 3: The Transplanting Technique ​Handle with Care: The CCN-51 has a sensitive taproot. Slice the polybag vertically and remove it without shaking or breaking the soil ball. ​Setting the Plant: Place the seedling so the soil level of the bag matches the ground level. Firm the soil around it to remove air pockets that could cause root rot. ​Timing: Transplant only during the cool of the morning or late afternoon to minimise moisture loss and heat stress. ​Phase 4: The First 24 Months (The Growth Phase) ​Nutrition: The CCN-51 is a "hungry" variety. Because it grows and fruits so fast, it requires a strict fertilisation schedule. Apply NPK in small, frequent doses rather than one large application. ​Pruning: This is the most important step for CCN-51. You must prune to maintain a "single stem" for the first 50-60cm before it branches. Pruning ensures the energy goes into fruit production and keeps the tree at a manageable height. ​Weed Control: In a 3m x 3m layout, you must keep the "cocoa floor" clean. Once the canopy closes (around year 3), the shade will naturally prevent most weed growth. ​Phase 5: Pest and Disease Monitoring ​While CCN-51 is resilient, you must stay vigilant. ​Mirids: Watch for these sap-sucking insects during the dry season. ​Black Pod: Even with resistance, ensure good drainage and airflow through proper pruning to minimise fungal pressure during heavy rains. ​The Goal: Early Canopy Closure ​By following this guide, your CCN-51 trees will touch branches by the end of Year 3. This "canopy closure" creates a self-sustaining microclimate that protects the soil, retains moisture, and signals the beginning of your peak production years.
